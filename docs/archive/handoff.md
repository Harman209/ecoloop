# ECOLOOP — Project Handoff

## 1. Project Overview

**Project name:** ECOLOOP  
**School:** Swami Sant Dass Public School, Jalandhar  
**Domain:** Circular economy / zero-waste management / waste-to-value  
**Current stage:** MVP / prototype + early market validation

ECOLOOP is a student-led circular-economy initiative that converts waste generated in and around the school into useful products instead of treating it only as waste.

Core framing:

> **ECOLOOP does not just manage waste. It creates value from waste.**

The project combines:
- source segregation
- waste processing
- reuse/upcycling
- product development
- student participation
- vocational skills
- a digital storefront/order-enquiry system
- a circular business model

The project should be presented as more than a physical waste-management model. The business angle is important: waste becomes an input, students help transform it into products, those products can be sold, and revenue can support continuation and expansion.

## 3. Core Concept

The underlying concept is a **circular economy**.

Instead of:

> Waste → disposal → landfill

ECOLOOP attempts to create:

> Waste → segregation → processing/treatment → recovery/upcycling → useful product → sale/use → value → reinvestment → continued circular system

The project communicates that materials can remain in use longer and that waste can become an economic resource.

Useful phrase:

> **Waste today, resource tomorrow.**

Another:

> **We don't just manage waste, we create value from it.**

Important: the **mesh pipeline filter is only a component of the process/model, not a product**.

Plastic bottles filled/stuffed with **non-recyclable plastic wrappers and similar plastic waste**.

Natural cleaners prepared from fruit and vegetable peels.

## 8. Business Vocabulary

Use business terminology only when it genuinely fits.

### Revenue
Money earned from product sales.

### COGS — Cost of Goods Sold
Direct cost associated with producing a product.

### Gross Profit
**Revenue − COGS**

### Gross Margin
**Gross Profit ÷ Revenue × 100**

### Operating Expenses / OpEx
Costs of running the initiative that are not directly tied to one individual product.

### Contribution Margin
**Selling price − variable cost per unit**

### Break-even Point
The sales level at which total revenue covers total costs.

### Unit Economics
The economics of producing and selling one unit.

Example:

> Selling Price − Unit COGS = contribution from one unit.

### ROI — Return on Investment
Return generated relative to money invested.

### Circular Business Model
A model where resources remain in use through reuse, recycling, recovery and upcycling.

### Waste-to-Value
Converting waste with little/no economic value into useful products with potential economic value.

### Upcycling
Turning discarded material into something useful, often with greater utility/value.

### Value Proposition
Why a customer should choose ECOLOOP.

Possible wording:

> **ECOLOOP converts difficult-to-manage waste into useful, affordable products while creating environmental and economic value.**

Do not use EBITDA just to sound sophisticated. At the current school-MVP stage, unit economics, COGS, margin, break-even and validation are more useful.

## 13. Scientific / Academic Basis From the Submitted Report

The report describes the project around:

### Source Segregation
Separating organic and inorganic/recyclable waste at the source to simplify recycling and composting.

### Waste Reduction
The report states that effective source segregation can reduce landfill waste by up to 50%, because organic waste can be diverted to composting or anaerobic digestion.

### Slurry Management
The report states that mesh pipeline filters can trap up to 80% of solid food particles in wastewater.

This is a **process component**, not a product.

### Compost & Biogas Potential
The report states:
- approximately 150–200 kg of compost per ton of waste
- approximately 60–80 cubic metres of gas per ton of food waste

These are figures from the submitted report. If using them as public scientific claims, verify the sources before presenting them as universal performance figures.

### Environmental Impact
The report says:
- recycling can reduce the need for raw-material extraction
- composting can reduce methane emissions compared with landfilling organic waste
